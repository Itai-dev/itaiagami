Internal. Not deployed (`docs` and `*.md` are in `.vercelignore`).

Six outlines. **None of these are written or published.** They are here so that

- A specific claim in the first two paragraphs, not a throat-clear.
- ~900–1,400 words. Four to six `<h2>` sections. One blockquote at most.
- No listicles, no "in today's fast-moving landscape", no invented numbers.

## 1. When a product launch needs a Creative Director, not more assets

**Target reader:** a founder or product lead about to brief a production studio.
**Claim:** if you can already name the deliverables, you have a production
problem; if you cannot, buying production first is how launches get expensive.

**Outline**
1. The symptom — a launch brief that arrives as a shopping list (hero film, three cutdowns, a landing page) with no sentence saying what the audience should now believe.
2. What production buys and what it does not: production makes the thing well; it does not decide whether the thing is worth making.
3. The Vision Pro case as the clearest example — a product on a platform with no visual conventions and no way for the audience to try it. There was nothing to produce until someone decided what "how it feels" meant. Link `work/simply-vision-pro.html`.
4. The Amdocs case as the other shape — a rebrand that needed a centrepiece, where the pinball decision came first and everything else followed from it. Link `work/amdocs-make-it-amazing.html`.
5. A test the reader can apply: can you write one sentence describing what changes in the audience's head? If not, you are not ready to produce.
6. Close: what direction costs relative to producing the wrong thing twice.

**Links out:** `services/creative-direction-product-launches.html`, `notes/how-to-brief-a-creative-director.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- One concrete moment on the Vision Pro project where the concept decision changed what was produced. Which direction was rejected, and why?
- Whether the Amdocs pinball idea was chosen against alternatives, and what those were (or confirmation that this cannot be published).
- Any launch where the brief arrived as a deliverable list and was successfully reopened — anonymised if the client cannot be named.

## 2. What an identity system needs beyond a logo

**Target reader:** a marketing lead who has been quoted "a logo and a style guide".
**Claim:** a logo is the cheapest part. What determines whether an identity
survives is the set of decisions that let people who were not in the room extend
it correctly.

**Outline**
1. Open on the real failure mode: an identity that looks right in the presentation and drifts within eighteen months.
2. The components that actually do the work — behaviour rules, hierarchy, motion logic, the negative decisions ("we never do this").
3. Channel 13 as the case: a national network where the same mark has to sit over news, prime-time and promotion, applied daily by people the designer will never meet. What went into the brand book and channel kit and why each part exists. Link `work/channel-13.html`.
4. Partner TV as the variant: three channels, three energies, one identity — what stays fixed and what is allowed to vary. Link `work/partner-tv.html`.
5. The governance question nobody asks in the pitch: who decides, after handover, when something is off-system?
6. Close: the test of a system is whether a stranger can extend it without asking.

**Links out:** `services/brand-strategy-identity-systems.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- The actual table of contents of the Channel 13 brand book — which sections exist. This is the spine of the piece and it cannot be written from memory of the case-study page.
- One rule in that book that was contested during the project, and how it was resolved.
- For Partner TV: what specifically was locked across the three channels versus left free.

## 3. How to scope an interactive museum experience

**Target reader:** a curator or museum project lead writing a tender.
**Claim:** most museum tenders specify hardware before deciding what a visitor
does — which fixes the cost and forfeits the experience.

**Outline**
1. The visitor as the actual constraint: arrives mid-thought, no context, children in tow, ninety seconds of attention. Everything is scoped against that person.
2. The order that works — visitor journey → interaction → content → medium → hardware. And what goes wrong when a tender starts at the end of that list.
3. Disciplines a real exhibition needs and the sequence they join in: curator and subject expert, illustrator, designer, animator, developer, integrator. Where each one's decisions become expensive to reverse.
4. Museum of Natural History: working alongside environmental-science experts so the material stays accurate while becoming explorable. Link `work/museum-of-natural-history.html`.
5. Tower of David: designing into a protected historic building — light, stone, sightlines, the constraints of the room. Link `work/tower-of-david.html`.
6. A scoping checklist a curator can lift straight into a brief.
7. Close: what to leave open in a tender so bidders can propose an idea rather than a price for your idea.

**Links out:** `services/interactive-immersive-experiences.html`, `notes/what-museums-get-wrong-about-technology.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- Realistic phase durations for an installed exhibit — concept, design, build, install — even as ranges. Without these the piece is advice, not experience.
- The specific architectural constraints that shaped the Tower of David work (which rooms, what they ruled out).
- How the collaboration with the science experts at the Museum of Natural History actually ran — review cadence, who had final say on accuracy.
- Anything that failed a visitor test and had to be redesigned. This would be the strongest paragraph in the piece.

## 4. What a Fractional Creative Director actually owns

**Target reader:** a CMO or VP Marketing weighing a full-time CD hire.
**Claim:** the value is not extra capacity. It is that someone is accountable for
the creative decision — and that the accountability is transferable to the team.

**Outline**
1. The situation: capable designers, motion people, writers — and reviews that are opinion-gathering because nobody owns the call.
2. What is owned: the standard, the territories, the review rhythm, the reasoning made explicit. What is not owned: headcount, roadmap, execution volume.
3. Why the reasoning matters more than the verdict — a decision without a stated reason teaches the team nothing and has to be re-made next week.
4. Natural Intelligence as the case: creative territories, hypotheses, the loop from tension to concept to variation to learning. Link `work/natural-intelligence.html`.
5. The exit condition: the engagement should get smaller. If it does not, something is being substituted for rather than built.
6. Honest comparison against the alternatives — full-time CD, agency of record, freelance designer. When each is the right purchase.

**Links out:** `services/fractional-creative-direction.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- What the working rhythm actually was at Natural Intelligence — how often reviews happened, who was in them, how a decision got recorded.
- A named example of a creative territory and the audience tension behind it (the case-study page describes the method but no single territory end to end).
- Whether he has run a genuinely fractional/retained engagement elsewhere, and on what terms. If not, the piece must be framed as the model he offers rather than one he has run repeatedly — that distinction has to be honest.

## 5. What a ₪30K–₪100K creative engagement includes

**Target reader:** anyone about to email and unsure whether their budget is serious.
**Claim:** the number does not buy hours, it buys scope. Saying it early is what
makes the conversation short.

**Outline**
1. Why this is worth writing down at all: most people guess, guess wrong, and either overpay or never write.
2. What ₪30K–₪60K realistically covers — the shape of an engagement at that level, and what it explicitly does not include.
3. What changes at ₪60K–₪120K.
4. Above ₪120K: multidisciplinary, team assembled, scoped separately, and why that is a different kind of agreement rather than the same one scaled up.
5. What always sits outside the number: third-party production, licensing, media, hardware.
6. How to write the first email so the range is useful rather than defensive.

**Links out:** `services.html`, `contact.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- **This piece cannot be written without him.** Every band needs real content: typical duration, what is delivered, how many rounds, what triggers a re-scope.
- Whether he wants to publish this level of specificity at all. It is the single highest-intent page the site could have, and also the one most likely to be quoted back at him in a negotiation. His call.

## 6. How creative systems let teams generate, test and scale ideas

**Target reader:** a growth or brand lead whose team ships a lot and learns little.
**Claim:** volume is not the problem and headcount is not the fix. The problem is
that most variations test nothing.

**Outline**
1. The failure: a hundred assets shipped, and afterwards nobody can say which idea was tested. Variation on the colour of a button is not an experiment.
2. Territories as the unit — distinct points of view with their own tone and visual language, instead of one house style stretched thin.
3. The loop: audience tension → hypothesis → territory → concept → variation → test → learning → next concept. Where it usually breaks.
4. Natural Intelligence as the worked case: entering each category through its own human tension — urgency in car insurance, care in pet insurance, complexity in lending. Link `work/natural-intelligence.html`.
5. Where AI belongs and where it does not: the AI-assisted resizing MVP removed repetitive adaptation while composition, hierarchy and creative intent stayed under a designer's judgment. The line matters more than the tooling.
6. What a team needs in place before a system like this is worth building.

**Links out:** `services/fractional-creative-direction.html`.

**NEEDS FROM ITAI**
- One full hypothesis → creative → result chain that is publishable. **No invented metrics** — if performance numbers cannot be shared, the piece must be written qualitatively and say so.
- What the resizing MVP actually did, technically, and what it deliberately refused to automate.
- One hypothesis that was wrong, and what the failure taught. This is the paragraph that would make the piece credible.
