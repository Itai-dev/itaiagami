How it works

Three steps

1. A 20-minute call

You tell me what is launching, who it is for and what is still undecided. No brief needed. If it is a fit, I send a short proposal for a Sprint within a few days.

2. The Sprint

Two weeks, fixed price. You see directions early and often, not one big reveal at the end. You leave with the idea, how it looks, and a plan for the rest.

3. The launch

If you go ahead, I lead the full launch with the team it needs, or stay on as your Creative Director. One point of accountability from first idea to launch day.

Common questions

Before you write
Do I need a finished brief before we talk?

No. Most projects start with an idea, a launch or a problem rather than a list of deliverables. Working out what the work needs to say, the central idea and who is needed to build it is usually the first phase.

Who do you usually work with?

Founders, CMOs, heads of brand and marketing and product leaders, usually at a company launching or changing something: a product, an app, a brand, a campaign, a space or a move into a new market. And creative studios and agencies that need a concept, art direction or another senior pair of hands. I work with the people making the decisions directly.

What kinds of projects are the best fit?

Projects where the idea still has to be found, and then has to be built: a new brand, a product launch, a campaign, a film, a digital experience or something people walk into. In practice that has meant technology companies and ambitious products, media and broadcast organisations, museums and cultural institutions, and organisations that need to explain something genuinely complex.

What is the typical starting budget?

A Launch Sprint starts at ₪30,000 in Israel, $10,000 in the US and Canada, €10,000 in Europe and £8,000 in the UK. Prices are set per market rather than converted. A Full Launch is scoped after the Sprint, with the team the work needs.

Why start with a Sprint?

Because the idea decides everything after it. A fixed-price Sprint lets you see the idea and how it looks before committing to a full production budget, and it gives us both a clear plan and a fair price for the rest.

How do you use AI?

As one tool among many. I use it to explore visual directions, test ideas, prototype motion and interaction, and build digital experiences, so more of an idea can be seen and tried early. It doesn’t replace taste or direction: the ideas and the final call are mine, and where the work needs specialists, I bring them in. Not every project uses it, and the case studies say where it was part of the work.

Do you take standalone design, motion or 3D projects?

Yes, when there is a good creative fit. Hands-on design, motion, 3D and digital work are part of the practice, as a standalone piece or as a contribution to a direction that already exists.

Can you work with a larger creative team?

Yes. I bring in designers, developers, animators, illustrators, 3D artists and other specialists when a project requires them. When I lead, I keep the central idea coherent across everything the team makes; when I join an established team, I work within its direction.

Do you work internationally?

Yes. I am based in Tel Aviv and work worldwide, remotely and on site where a project needs it.
